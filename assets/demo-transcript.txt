FARE SIGNAL: A NARRATED DEMO
Made-up data. No real Telegram messages.

A ticket below €40
I needed a ticket below forty euros. The fare for my first choice kept rising, so I built Fare Signal to watch later direct trains.

The whole journey, safely simulated
The original private watcher ran about every two hours and sent real Telegram alerts. This public walkthrough uses made-up data and sends no real messages.

Make a bot. Press Start.
First, I make a pretend bot and press Start. The chat now has a place for its replies.

Two hours in one click
I start the watch. One click moves the pretend clock forward by two hours and runs the next check.

Same train. Same price.
The same journey and price must agree in two reads. The fare must be strictly below forty euros.

€34.90 is below €40
A direct train at thirty four euros and ninety cents passes. Its alert appears in the Telegram-style chat, with the journey and price.

No repeat interruptions
I check again. No new messages appear. The notebook remembers the trains it already sent.

Different prices? Wait.
If the two prices disagree, the system waits. A cheap number is not enough.

Ticket bought. Watch off.
Once I have a ticket, I stop the watch. It cannot run again until I start over.

A later train worked for me
The real project helped me find a suitable later train. The public demo lets anyone explore the whole idea with made-up data.
